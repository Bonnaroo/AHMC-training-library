# Stormwater Awareness and Spill Control Training Notes

## Training Title

Stormwater Awareness and Spill Control

## Purpose

This training helps employees understand how to prevent spills, leaks, oils, coolants, chemicals, and contaminated water from reaching storm drains, floor drains, outside drains, soil, or retention ponds.

Stormwater protection is important because anything that enters an outside storm drain may reach the environment without treatment.

## Who Needs This Training

This training applies to employees, supervisors, maintenance employees, contractors, and anyone who works around chemicals, oils, coolants, waste containers, scrap areas, docks, outdoor storage areas, or storm drains.

## Key Awareness Points

- Storm drains are for rainwater only.
- Do not dump liquids into storm drains, floor drains, outside drains, soil, or retention ponds.
- Oil, coolant, chemicals, dirty mop water, wash water, and unknown liquids must not be allowed to enter drains.
- Containers must be kept closed when not in use.
- Leaking containers, totes, drums, bins, or equipment must be reported.
- Outdoor areas must be kept clean and free of spills, residue, trash, and liquid leaks.
- Scrap bins, waste containers, and chemical containers must not leak onto the ground.
- Spill kits must be used when needed.
- Employees should stop the source of a spill only if it is safe to do so.
- Employees must report spills, leaks, or unusual liquids immediately.

## What Employees Should Do

Employees are expected to:

- Watch for leaks, spills, and unusual liquids.
- Keep liquids away from drains.
- Never dump anything into a storm drain.
- Use spill materials if trained and it is safe.
- Notify a supervisor, maintenance, or EHS when a spill or leak is found.
- Keep containers closed and stored correctly.
- Report damaged drums, totes, bins, or containers.
- Keep outdoor areas clean and free of loose materials.

## What Supervisors Should Verify

Supervisors should verify that:

- Employees know not to dump anything into storm drains.
- Spill kits are available and accessible.
- Leaks and spills are reported quickly.
- Outdoor waste and scrap areas are checked regularly.
- Containers are closed and in good condition.
- Any spill cleanup is completed and communicated.
- EHS is notified when there is a spill, leak, or possible environmental concern.

## Spill Control Basics

If a spill happens:

- Stop the source if it is safe.
- Keep the spill away from drains.
- Use absorbent materials or spill kit supplies if trained.
- Notify a supervisor, maintenance, or EHS.
- Do not wash the spill into a drain.
- Dispose of used cleanup materials properly.
- Report the spill even if it has already been cleaned up.

## Examples of Things to Report

Employees should report:

- Oil or coolant on the floor
- Leaking drums, totes, or containers
- Leaking scrap bins
- Liquid near storm drains
- Milky, oily, or discolored water outside
- Unknown liquid on the ground
- Damaged spill kits
- Chemical containers stored outside approved areas
- Trash containers or scrap containers leaking liquid

## Important Reminder

When in doubt, report it. It is better to report a small issue early than to miss something that could become a larger environmental problem.

## Quiz Questions

### Question 1

What are storm drains for?

A. Any liquid waste
B. Rainwater only
C. Coolant and oil
D. Mop water

**Correct answer: B**

### Question 2

What should you do if you see oil, coolant, or an unknown liquid near a drain?

A. Ignore it
B. Wash it down the drain
C. Report it and keep it away from the drain if safe
D. Cover it with trash

**Correct answer: C**

### Question 3

Can employees dump dirty water, coolant, oil, or chemicals into a storm drain?

A. Yes
B. No

**Correct answer: B**

### Question 4

What should be done with leaking drums, totes, scrap bins, or containers?

A. Leave them alone
B. Report them
C. Push them outside
D. Put them near a drain

**Correct answer: B**

### Question 5

If a spill happens, what is one of the most important things to do?

A. Keep it away from drains
B. Hide it
C. Wash it outside
D. Wait until the next shift

**Correct answer: A**
